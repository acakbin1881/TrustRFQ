<objective>
Close the two failure paths that stand between a validated quote and a settled swap: the taker who
cannot receive the maker's token yet, and the quote whose authorization expires while the taker is
looking at the wallet. Both are handled without ever asking for a signature at a price the taker
has not seen.

Purpose: the taker-side guarantee this whole protocol sells is that within a quote's validity the
price cannot change — the signature binds exact amounts and the chain settles exactly those or
nothing. After expiry the old price is dead, and only a re-shown price may be signed. An
unconditional auto-retry would quietly break that promise while every individual step still looked
correct, so the retry here is price-guarded.

Output: `src/core/rfq/retry.ts` (pure decision logic), the trustline pre-flight and in-flow prompt,
the guarded retry in the accept path, and the driver scenarios that prove both.
</objective>

  <action>
Write `src/core/rfq/retry.ts` as a pure module: no network, no globals, no wallet. It encodes two
rules and nothing else.

`needsTrustline(balances, tokenStr)` is the D-08 predicate over the already-fetched balance map.
Three distinct states must stay distinct: the native asset never needs one; a null map means the
answer is not yet known and the caller must not be told a trustline is missing; a map that has been
fetched and lacks the key means the trustline is genuinely absent. Zero balance with a present key
is a live trustline. This mirrors the fail-closed posture `canAfford` already takes in
`src/core/balances.ts` — unknown is never resolved into a convenient answer.

`isExpiredAuthFailure(err)` classifies a settlement failure as an authorization-expiration failure
or not. Match on the error text the host and the RPC actually produce rather than on a broad
substring that would swallow unrelated authorization problems: an insufficient balance, a paused
contract, or a cancelled order must all classify false, because retrying those would be pointless
and would burn the one retry the guard allows.

`retryDecision({ failure, seenPrice, freshQuote, attemptsAlready })` returns exactly one of
`auto_retry`, `reconfirm`, or `stop`, with the reason attached. The ordering of its guards is the
security property: `stop` when the failure is not an authorization expiration; `stop` when a retry
has already been attempted; `stop` when no fresh quote came back; then compare prices with
`quotePrice` from `src/core/rfq/discover.ts` so both sides use one comparison — `auto_retry` when
the fresh price is equal or better, `reconfirm` when it is strictly worse. Equal is deliberately
on the auto side: the taker has already seen and agreed to that exact price, so re-showing it would
be friction with no consent value, while a worse price is by definition a price they have not seen.

Write `src/core/rfq/retry.test.ts` covering every behavior line, with the equal-price boundary and
the at-most-once property as named, explicit tests rather than incidental coverage.

## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| panel -> wallet prompt | The consent surface. The wallet's own contract-invocation prompt does not render amounts human-readably, so what the panel showed is what the taker actually agreed to |
| maker's re-quote -> retry decision | A fresh price from the same untrusted server, arriving at the moment the taker's attention has already moved on |
| Horizon balances -> trustline note | Cached, possibly-unfetched data used to make a claim about the taker's account |
